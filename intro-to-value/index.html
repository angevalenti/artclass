Visual Art I · Shape & Value

Intro to Value

Light and dark are what make a flat drawing look real.

Let the sphere sit there and ask: is that drawing flat or round? How can you tell?

Look closely

These are drawings. Why do they look real?

Give them 60 seconds to look. Ask: what makes these look shiny and 3D? Steer away from color. It's the light and dark: bright white highlights right next to very dark shadows. Tell them: by the end of this unit, you will draw one of these.

Vocabulary

Value

How light or dark something is. Not the color. Just light and dark.

Lightest light

Darkest dark

Quick check: hold up a white paper and a black sweatshirt. Which has the lighter value? Then a yellow and a navy object. Squint: you see value, not color.

Vocabulary

Contrast

The difference between the lights and the darks.

High contrast

Whitest whites next to blackest blacks. Looks bold and 3D.

Low contrast

Everything is a middle gray. Looks soft and flat.

Same sphere, same shape. The only thing that changed is the range of values. Ask: which one looks more real? Then: which one looks like your drawings usually look? Most beginners stop at low contrast. Push for the darks.

The five parts of light

How light wraps a form

Highlight: where the light hits directly. Brightest spot.

Midtones: the light and shadow grays as the form turns.

Core shadow: the darkest band on the object itself.

Reflected light: light bouncing back near the edge.

Cast shadow: the shadow the object throws on the table.

Walk through each label on the drawing. Point out that the core shadow is NOT at the very edge: reflected light sits between it and the edge. Cast shadow is darkest right where the sphere touches the table. Notice the value scales up top: the artist matched the sphere's values to them.

Value vocabulary

Value

How light or dark something is.

Contrast

The difference between lights and darks.

Highlight

The brightest spot, where light hits.

Midtone

The grays between light and dark.

Core shadow

The darkest band on the object.

Reflected light

Light bouncing back near the edge.

Cast shadow

The shadow it throws on the table.

Hatching

Parallel lines. Closer = darker.

Cross-hatching

Lines that cross. More layers = darker.

Stippling

Dots. More dots = darker.

Quick review before studio time. Point to a term and have students point to it on their own drawing. Teal circles mark the lit side, purple circles mark the shadow side.

Activity 1 · 15 minutes

Make two value scales

A. Pattern scale

Pick one mark: lines, cross-hatching or dots. Go from spread out to packed tight across 5 boxes.

B. Smooth scale

Even pressure, no visible lines. Push for a true black.

Point out every row on the example. The top two are smooth shading, stepped and blended. The next two are hatching and cross-hatching. The bottom is stippling. Same idea each time: marks packed tight for dark, spread out for light. When both are done, ask: which looks more realistic? Which has more personality?

Activity 2 · Rest of class

From circle to sphere

1

Circle + light

Draw a light circle. Arrow = light.

2

Highlight

Outline it lightly. Keep it white.

3

First layer

Shade softly, away from the light.

4

Midtones

Build grays around the curve.

5

Core shadow

Go darker. Save a light rim.

6

Cast shadow

Blend, then ground it on the table.

Check yourself: a clear highlight, a true dark, all five parts of light?

Walk the room. The two most common problems: no real dark (push them to press harder or layer), and a highlight that wanders because they forgot their light source. Step 2 is key: outline the highlight first so they don't shade over it.

Done early?

Try a new form

Cube

Three faces, three values: light, middle, dark.

Cone

Shade in strips from tip to base.

Flip the light

Same sphere, light from the other side.

Exit ticket

Label the five parts of light on your sphere.

Fast finishers pick one form. On the cube, point out that each face is one flat value. On the cone, value changes side to side, not top to bottom. Collect exit tickets at the door.
